## Starting from a CSV and a Kaggle account

The starting state is the common one: an exported CSV, a Kaggle account, a laptop with no GPU, and an instruction to show results next month.

The end state: a forecast produced automatically at seven every morning for the following day and written back to the energy platform, with operators seeing forecast, baseline and actual together on a dashboard.

## Day by day

| Days | Task | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Data health check: plot, gaps, timestamps, confirm the zeros with the site | Gaps classified, suspect points under 8%, at least 6 shutdown examples |
| 3 | Establish the naive baselines | A MASE pass mark in hand |
| 4–5 | Features and the time-ordered backtest frame | Leakage checklist all green, eight-fold backtest running |
| 6–7 | First LightGBM and a rolling backtest | Multi-fold mean significantly better than the baseline |
| 8 | Zero-shot foundation-model comparison | One comparable number |
| 9–10 | Tuning or fine-tuning — where Kaggle earns its place | Another 0.5 points or keep version one |
| 11 | Quantiles and thresholds | Nominal 80% interval covering 75–85% |
| 12 | Inference service and degradation paths | All three fallbacks triggerable by hand |
| 13 | Plant integration, end to end | Idempotent write verified |
| 14 | Shadow run and hand over | All three lines on the dashboard, archiving started |

Every day carries an explicit acceptance criterion. **Failing one means stopping, not pushing on.** A plan that cannot stop delivers, on day fourteen, a model nobody can defend.

## The highest delay risk is never the modelling

The bench is a resource-constrained plan model. Change the available person-days, whether a GPU exists, the data-quality grade and how responsive the site is, and watch fourteen days become twenty.

The risk ranking comes out almost always the same.

1. **Days 1–2, the data health check.** It stretches because you are waiting for the site to confirm whether those zeros are shutdowns or dropouts (DT2), and the site has its own rota.
2. **Day 13, the plant integration.** Interfaces, permissions and a shutdown window all have to be queued for, and none of the three is under your control.

The modelling days are the most predictable of all, because they **depend only on you**.

That conclusion has a direct effect on planning: start everything involving the site as early as possible — book the integration window on day one — rather than letting it sit at position thirteen in a linear sequence.

## Data quality is the largest lever

Drag "how messy the data is" from 12% to 100% on the bench and fourteen days becomes more than twenty, with every extra day landing in the first row.

That is why this book puts the DT module ahead of every model: on data that was never cleaned properly, the following twelve days of work are systematically discounted.

## The no-GPU cell

If the tuning or fine-tuning on days 9–10 sits on the critical path and you have no GPU, that step is stretched by Kaggle's quota and session wall.

Renting a card for the price of a lunch is then the highest-return money in the entire plan — PF3 already did that arithmetic. But go back to PF1 first and confirm you **actually** need that step. Many projects have already met their acceptance criteria by day seven.

## Exercises

1. Run the plan model with your own parameters: how many person-days, how messy the data, GPU or not?
2. Identify every step in your project that is not under your control and move its start date earlier.
3. Turn the fourteen acceptance criteria into a checklist and put it somewhere visible.
4. After the project, compare: how many days did it actually take, where was the largest delay, and did this model predict it?
