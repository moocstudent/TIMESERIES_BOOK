## One fifteen-minute window a month is billed

The plant: thirty-two injection-moulding machines, two air compressors, a central chiller, a 2000 kVA transformer, on a two-part tariff. The capacity charge is billed on maximum demand — the largest fifteen-minute sliding window in the month — at forty-two yuan per kilowatt.

That sentence contains the whole chapter's tension: **of 2,880 fifteen-minute windows in a month, exactly one decides the capacity charge**.

@fig t29-ledger

## That fact rewrites the modelling objective

If only one window is billed, then:

- average error is nearly worthless;
- what pays is **peak-timing hit rate** and **the deviation of the estimated monthly peak**.

MAPE is not your metric. EV2's "priced by consequence" has its most literal expression here.

## The whole chain

**Data**: the plant's fifteen-minute meter registers — interval means, matching the billing basis exactly. Had they been point samples, everything downstream would be wasted (DT1). Plus the production schedule and a temperature forecast.

**Baseline**: last-week same-slot median, with 6.8% deviation on the estimated monthly demand.

**Model**: LightGBM with schedule features plus a P90 quantile model (ML3), cutting deviation to 2.4%.

**Control**: when the day-ahead forecast shows a window approaching the demand target, stagger the two compressors' loading and delay a batch of non-urgent machines by twenty minutes — about 140 kilowatts.

One thing is easy to get wrong here: **real demand control does not defend a line, it defends the month's running maximum**. Acting on a window that cannot set a new record saves nothing and still costs you the deferred production. The bench's controller therefore acts only when `actual > max(target, current billed maximum − 15)`.

## Count the ledger honestly

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Demand charge saved | +¥3,876 |
| Production lost to false triggers | −¥1,600 |
| Monthly net benefit | ¥2,276 |
| One-off build cost | ¥32,000 |
| Payback | about 14 months |

The false-trigger line must be counted. Production deferred for nothing costs real money, and it is directly tied to forecast accuracy: the lower the hit rate, the more false triggers. A ledger that omits it is fiction.

## The counter-intuitive conclusion

Run two comparisons on the bench:

- lift peak-timing accuracy from 70% to 85%;
- improve MAPE from 6% to 4%.

The second is far harder — a new model, more features, more tuning, possibly a rented card. The first often needs no more than switching the loss to pinball, weighting peak-hour samples, or connecting the production schedule.

And the first is worth considerably more, because **billing only ever looks at that one window**.

This is the same point SG2 and EV2 kept making: fix the consequence first, let it choose the metric, and do not let a tutorial's default MSE decide for you.
