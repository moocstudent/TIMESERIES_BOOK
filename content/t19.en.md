## Measure, then decide

This module answers where to train, but the first chapter must answer something upstream: **does it need a GPU at all?**

Skipping that has concrete costs — renting a card for a LightGBM job a laptop finishes in three minutes, or conversely grinding a three-day fine-tune on a laptop.

## First, the data size

| Setting | Rows |
|---|---|
| One tag, 15-minute, 3 years | 105,000 |
| Twenty tags, 3 years | 2.1 million |
| A global model over a thousand tags, 3 years | 100 million |

Only the last line approaches a scale where hardware deserves serious thought. Most industrial projects stop at the first.

## Then the experiment size: you buy the product

This is the most common budgeting error. People price a single run and forget they will do three hundred.

```
total compute = families x hyperparameter sets x folds x time per run
```

Three families × forty sets × five folds = 600 runs. At fourteen minutes each that is 140 hours. That product is what you are actually buying.

## Three classes of model, compared

| Family | One run, 100k rows × 50 features | GPU speed-up | VRAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear / ridge | Seconds | 1× | 0 |
| LightGBM | Seconds to minutes | 1.6× | 0 |
| Small TCN/LSTM | Minutes | 22× | 3 GB |
| Fine-tune a foundation model | Tens of hours | 60× | 14 GB |

The first two have **no use for a GPU at all**: trees and linear models are bound by memory bandwidth rather than arithmetic, and at this scale the GPU build is frequently slower once data movement is counted.

The bench's default configuration — one tag, three years, fifty features, LightGBM, forty hyperparameter sets across five folds — computes the whole 600-run plan at **12 seconds** of CPU.

The genuine watershed is the last row: fine-tuning a 200M foundation model takes tens of hours on CPU and hours on GPU.

## Do not trust an estimate you did not measure

This chapter's third tab is a benchmark script, for a simple reason: those throughput constants were measured on somebody else's machine with somebody else's data.

Run the smallest real case on your own hardware — LightGBM on 100k rows by 50 features, a small MLP on the same data — and put the constants back into the estimator. It takes under five minutes and it gives every later budget conversation an evidential basis.

## Two numbers

If the arithmetic really does call for a card, carry two numbers into the next chapter:

1. **how much VRAM** (from DL4's estimator)
2. **how many hours** (from this chapter's product)

They are the entire input to the platform choice in PF2 and PF3: whether it fits a free T4, and whether it will hit a nine-hour session wall.

## Exercises

1. Measure your machine's real constants with this chapter's benchmark and put them into the estimator.
2. Multiply out your experiment matrix. How many runs in total?
3. Compute CPU and GPU hours for each model family and find where the line falls.
4. If the conclusion is that you do not need a GPU, spend the saved time on modules DT and FE — that is where this project's bottleneck actually is.
