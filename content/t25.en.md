## Grid search is almost always the worst choice

A counter-intuitive fact, and the reason is **the curse of dimensionality plus the observation that most hyperparameters do not matter**.

Of five hyperparameters, typically one or two drive the result. Grid search spends a large share of its budget resolving the unimportant dimensions: sixty trials spread over four axes is two or three points per axis. Random search projects sixty distinct values onto every axis, resolving the ones that matter far more finely.

@fig t25-search

On the bench with the same sixty trials: grid 12.8%, random 9.3%. That gap is structural rather than lucky.

Bayesian methods (TPE, GP) start to win once the budget is larger — beyond about fifty trials — and each run is expensive, because they concentrate new samples in promising regions.

## But read the test scores alongside

This is the chapter's real point. The bench's three searches, on validation and on test:

| Search | Best validation score | That configuration's test score |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | 10.4% | 10.3% |
| Random | 9.2% | 10.7% |
| TPE | 8.2% | 10.4% |

**The method that won by the most on validation did not win on the segment that never took part in any choice.**

That is **validation overfitting**: part of why TPE's validation score is lowest is that it searched harder than the others for luck in the same window. As the trial count climbs from 4 to 60, validation grows about 5.8 points more optimistic than test.

The more you search, the more of your winner's margin is luck.

## Three antidotes

**Select on the mean across folds, not a single fold.** The cheapest and most effective. A multi-fold mean has far lower variance and luck rarely strikes several folds at once. Change Optuna's objective from one fold to a four-fold rolling mean and the overfitting shrinks visibly.

**Keep a final test segment that takes part in no selection.** You may look at it once. After that you may not tune anything on it, or it is contaminated too.

**Do not take the single best point; average the near-optimal region.** A good part of the single best trial's margin is luck, and the mean of the top five parameters is usually steadier.

## Reproducibility: five lines that decide everything

Finally, the minimum viable experiment log. On every run record:

1. the code version (git hash)
2. the data version (file hash or dataset version)
3. the random seed
4. the full parameter dictionary
5. every metric

Append it as one JSON line to a file. No experiment platform is required, and it is the difference between experiments you can reproduce and a pile of good scores you will never see again.

Three months from now somebody asks where that 6.1% came from. Either you can answer in two minutes or you cannot. There is no third case.
